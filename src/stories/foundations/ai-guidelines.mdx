# AI assistance in Aegis

AI output is visibly generated, grounded in the supplied context, and subject to analyst review. Keep the evidence and the analyst's next action close to the response.

## Recognizable in every treatment

Use `--gradient-ai` for prominent AI actions, `--gradient-ai-soft` for secondary controls and chips, `--gradient-ai-border` for tertiary button outlines, `--gradient-ai-text` for AI labels and icons, and `--gradient-ai-surface` for generated content. The magenta-to-pink-to-blue gradient works in both themes. The shared AI marker keeps its star still while the internal gradient moves slowly; reduced-motion preference keeps the gradient static. AI cards and highlights use a uniform 1px border. Pair the marker with the explicit “AI generated” label; color alone must never identify a response as generated.

## Confidence is evidence strength

High, medium, and low confidence describe the strength and completeness of the available evidence. They do not replace severity, an analyst's disposition, or permission to apply a change. Include provenance and state what remains unverified.

## Keep progress inspectable

Stream readable markdown while leaving the interface usable. A caret indicates incoming text, a thinking indicator explains the current stage, and a collapsible working section lists actual tool steps with pending, working, complete, or error status. Announce state transitions rather than every incoming token. Respect reduced motion by disabling caret blinking, shimmer, rotating status messages, and decorative motion.

<ThinkingIndicator
  messages={['Reviewing the selected evidence', 'Preparing analyst verification steps']}
/>

Stop generation immediately when requested and preserve partial output. Show recoverable failures inline with a retry action. Copy, feedback, evidence links, and regenerate actions remain keyboard accessible. A stop before the first text token still offers regeneration. Return focus to the composer when a Stop or Regenerate control is replaced. Rendering markdown never executes raw HTML from a generated response.

## Show scope and provenance

The panel's context chips show which alerts, rules, events, and time scope the assistant can access. Evidence chips open the referenced record through the host application's citation callback or its supplied URL. Do not claim that labels alone prove a shared attack sequence. The local streaming mock explicitly distinguishes supplied references from queried telemetry.

## Preserve the context of each turn

Snapshot the prompt and attached context when a turn starts. Changing the visible selection affects the next request, while an existing turn and its citations keep their original scope. Regeneration uses that saved scope. A new keyed request may replace an active stream; cancel the old stream and ignore any late events, even if the adapter does not honor cancellation. Closing the panel stops its active generation and keeps the transcript available when reopened.

The composer sends with Enter and inserts a new line with Shift+Enter. The slash-command menu fills a draft for review; it does not send automatically. Attaching or removing context changes the visible chips. Failed sends retain the draft, and IME composition must finish before Enter can submit.

## Keep consequential decisions with the analyst

AI may propose detection logic, summarise an investigation, or suggest the next verification step. Applying a proposed rule requires an explicit review with the current and proposed YAML, approve and reject actions, and an audit line. A suggestion, confidence badge, or generated summary never applies an action by itself.

All examples stream deterministic local responses. They use no API keys, make no model requests, and do not execute security actions.
